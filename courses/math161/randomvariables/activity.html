Probability Distributions Activity

This activity will ask a question about any of the 5 probability distributions we have learned about: binomial, geometric, Poisson, uniform, and normal. The reason they are all combined into a single activity is that one of the greatest difficulties students face in working with these is not in the how to compute things, but rather in deciding which probability distribution applies to the situation. Some things to look out for:

  • Are Bernoulli attempts (something with a fixed probability, either given in the problem or that can be easily computed) being performed in the problem? If so, then you are probably looking at a binomial or geometric distribution.
  • If there are a fixed number of Bernoulli attempts performed, it is a binomial distribution.
  • If Bernoulli attempts are performed until a success, it is a geometric distribution.
  • Watch out for the words "on average/average rate". This may be a Poisson distribution.
  • If outcomes in an interval are "equally likely/uniformly distributed", this may be a uniform distribution.
  • The only circumstance in which you are looking at a normal distribution is if it is explicitly stated in the problem.
That last point takes all the guesswork out of determining whether you've got a normal distribution on your hands: you need only distinguish between the remaining 4. With enough practice, you learn to recognize these quickly.

Once you've identified the distribution in the problem, you will want to give its probability distribution function, and then use it to solve the problem. You may be asked for a probability of a particular outcome or range of outcomes, a conditional probability, a percentile/quartile... anything that has come up before is fair game!
